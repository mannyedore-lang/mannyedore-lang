# Enterprise Financial Services, Risk & Sustainability Case Studies

This portfolio section highlights selected transformation work across financial services, insurance, energy and enterprise data governance. It is written as professional experience context; public company facts are included only to establish the scale and operating environment, and no confidential client data is disclosed.

## USAA — Financial Advice & Solutions Group Vendor Selection and Wealth Management Desktop

Supported a Financial Advice & Solutions Group technology/vendor-selection initiative at USAA, helping evaluate capabilities intended to improve the advisor and member experience and support the broader wealth-management operating model.

My work connected business requirements, advisor workflows, technology capabilities, portfolio governance and vendor evaluation. I helped structure requirements and selection criteria, coordinate stakeholder input and assess solution fit across integration, data, workflow, usability, scalability, security, implementation complexity and long-term operating value.

The portfolio also included work associated with creating and managing a **wealth management desktop**—a consolidated advisor-oriented experience intended to bring relevant member/customer information, financial context, workflow, service activity and supporting tools into a more unified workspace. From a program and portfolio perspective, this required roadmap definition, dependency management, prioritization, vendor coordination, integration planning, delivery governance, risk management and executive visibility.

Representative responsibilities:

- Financial Advice & Solutions Group requirements and stakeholder alignment
- vendor evaluation, scoring and selection governance
- advisor/user journey and workflow analysis
- wealth-management desktop roadmap and portfolio management
- integration and data-dependency planning
- business/technology prioritization
- risk, issue and dependency governance
- implementation sequencing and release planning
- executive reporting and decision support

## ExxonMobil — Corporate Emissions & Data Reporting Schedule

Created and coordinated a corporate emissions and environmental-data reporting schedule in an ExxonMobil operating environment, connecting reporting obligations, data owners, source systems, validation activities, review gates and submission milestones.

The objective was to make emissions reporting a governed, repeatable data process rather than a last-minute reporting exercise. The schedule established clear ownership and cadence for data collection, quality review, reconciliation, management approval and reporting deliverables, with dependencies visible across participating functions.

The work included:

- emissions/environmental data collection calendar
- reporting milestones and due-date governance
- source-data owner and reviewer alignment
- data-quality and reconciliation checkpoints
- exception and late-data escalation paths
- management review and approval gates
- dependency tracking across reporting teams
- schedule/status reporting and audit-ready traceability

Public context: ExxonMobil reported **122 million metric tons of net equity CO2-equivalent greenhouse-gas emissions in 2015**, down approximately 1 million metric tons from 2014. Its published methodology referenced API and IPIECA industry guidance. This public information provides context for the scale and discipline required around corporate emissions data; it is not presented as a claim that I personally produced those corporate totals.

### State Farm in 2015 — Operating Context

State Farm was approaching its 94th year in 2015, having been founded in 1922 by Illinois farmer and insurance salesman **George Jacob “G.J.” Mecherle**. Its mutual-company heritage meant policyholders, rather than public shareholders, sat at the center of the enterprise model.

2015 was also a major leadership-transition year. Long-serving Chairman and CEO **Edward B. Rust Jr.**, who had served as CEO for roughly 30 years, announced that **Michael L. Tipsord** would succeed him as CEO in September 2015. Rust continued as Chairman through the transition; Tipsord was Vice Chairman, President and CEO in the 2015 annual report and later became Chairman in 2016.

State Farm's 2015 financial scale provides useful context for enterprise asset and risk work:

- **State Farm group combined net worth:** approximately **$82.7 billion** at year-end 2015, up from $80.0 billion in 2014.
- **State Farm group total revenue:** approximately **$75.7 billion** in 2015.
- **State Farm group net income:** approximately **$6.2 billion** in 2015.
- **State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company total assets:** approximately **$138.495 billion** at year-end 2015.
- **State Farm Bank assets:** approximately **$16.7 billion** at year-end 2015.
- **Retail mutual-fund assets under management:** approximately **$11.3 billion** at year-end 2015.
- **Life insurance in force:** approximately **$844.8 billion** at year-end 2015.

These figures are different measures and should not be combined as though they represented a single company valuation. State Farm is a mutual insurer, so conventional public-company market capitalization is not the appropriate valuation measure. The **$82.7 billion combined group net worth** is the more meaningful public financial-strength measure for the group in 2015, while the **$138.495 billion figure** represents total assets specifically reported by State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company.

State Farm reported that its property-casualty companies had a **$2.1 billion underwriting loss** on **$58.6 billion of earned premium** in 2015, while investment and other income helped produce P&C pre-tax operating profit. The company also maintained A.M. Best's A++ financial-strength rating for State Farm Mutual in its 2015 annual report.

For an asset and risk management program, that environment matters: a century-scale insurer with enormous financial assets, insurance obligations, technology estates, data, customer operations and regulatory responsibilities requires disciplined inventory, ownership, risk prioritization and roadmap governance.

## Portfolio Theme

Across USAA, ExxonMobil and State Farm, the common thread is turning complex enterprise information into governed decisions: selecting the right technology, creating portfolio roadmaps, establishing ownership, building reporting schedules, understanding risk and giving executives a reliable view of what requires action.
